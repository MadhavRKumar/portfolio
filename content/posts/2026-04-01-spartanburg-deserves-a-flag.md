> "...there is a broader layer of top-down municipal efforts that serve to create a
specific civic identity. If you ask us, the best place to start is a good flag."
> 
> -- 99% Invisible City

Like many others, I was introduced to the world of vexilology by [Roman Mars](https://www.ted.com/talks/roman_mars_why_city_flags_may_be_the_worst_designed_thing_you_ve_never_noticed)
and [99% Invisible.](https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/vexillonaire/) 
Recently, I was reading the 99% Invisible City and came across
this quote and it re-sparked my interest. Which leads me to the point of this post:
**Spartanburg deserves a flag.**

I love my city and have been so excited about all the cool things we're doing.
As we continue to grow, I believe we need something to help unify our identity.
Especially in our deeply divided world. That's why I think our city should come
together and adopt an official flag.

## Process

Our flag should be by the people, for the people. We should do something similar
to what [Columbia, SC](https://www.flagofcolumbia.com/process) did for their flag.

 1. Collect designs from the public.
 2. Consult with [NAVA](https://nava.org/) to narrow down submissions.
 3. Get public input.
 4. Have city council vote!

Then, we get to adopt a flag and you can bet I'll be buying one immediately! To
action on this, I'm going to [contact my city councilperson](https://www.cityofspartanburg.org/316/City-Council)
requesting that they adopt a resolution to open a design competition for our city
flag. If you're in Spartanburg and like this idea, I encourage you to do the same.

If you don't live in Spartanburg you should see if your city has a flag or if it
could use an update. I encourage you to see if your city would adopt a similar resolution!

### Colors

I chose blue, gold, and white for my designs. The navy blue and white come straight from 
the SC state flag. The lighter blue and gold come from the [Spartan Regiment flag.](https://bloximages.newyork1.vip.townnews.com/postandcourier.com/content/tncms/assets/v3/editorial/2/11/2118bdd0-e2d3-4caf-a682-f1f5f1151d49/68234dec07923.image.jpg?resize=1035%2C1099)[^1]

### Symbols

I was trying to evoke ideas of "Hub City" and "Sparkle City" which is why you see
so many hub and spoke and star designs. Additionally, I liked the convergence of
colors to lean into the hub idea.

You'll also notice I use 7 pointed stars and 7 stripes. This is to reference the
[7 major railway lines](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=273355) that inspired 
the "Hub City" nickname.

[^1]: The Hub City Spartanburgers are similarly honoring the [Spartan Regiment flag](https://www.sccsc.edu/news/2026/spartanburgers-honor-upstates-military-history-with-spartan-regiment-alternate-identity.html) with an alternate identity. Relatedly,
seeing [their flag](https://milbstore.com/collections/hub-city-spartanburgers/products/hub-city-spartanburgers-3x5-deluxe-flag)
all over Hampton Heights and Converse Heights is part of what made me realize
there's a demand for a sense of shared symbolic identity.
